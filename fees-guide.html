Fees

Maintenance fees and more...

Annual fees go toward the upkeep of the entire resort — here's a line-by-line look at where that money actually goes.

Every year, around the beginning of October, maintenance fee (MF) bills start going out to owners. We all know it's coming, but a lot of owners get genuinely upset around this time and take their frustration out in groups and forums online. MFs pay to maintain and operate the resorts, so they're a necessity — but that doesn't make them fun to receive.

What's on the bill

The bill arrives by postal mail, with the option to switch to eBilling and electronic notification. It's very high-level and doesn't go into the detailed breakdown of what's actually covered. Most commonly, the bill includes an Operating Fee and a Replacement Reserve. Some resorts also include a line item for property taxes, utilities, and other items, either due to local requirements or resort-specific decisions. There may also be an optional line item for ARDA-ROC — the American Resort Development Association's Resort Owners Coalition, a legislative lobby group. Whether you donate to it is up to you.

Where to find the full details

For owners who want to know exactly what they're paying for, the full budget is normally mailed out as well, and should also be available by logging into your owner account and looking for the Maintenance Fee link under Account. There's a link to the full budget with every line item — that's where you can see the details of all expenses and fees.

Why fees rise

When you think about why MFs rise most years, consider the household expenses you already deal with — insurance, maintenance, savings to cover the unexpected. Timeshare resorts have to cover very similar categories of expense. Specific to the Replacement Reserve: that's a fund used for major refurbishment of the resort, which happens on a schedule — every 5, 10, and/or 15 years, depending on the resort's guidelines.

Club Dues

Club Dues is a separate expense specific to anyone who owns points or has a week enrolled in the points program. This fee runs a few hundred dollars a year and depends on which ownership level you're at. It covers a corporate Interval account for your points and enrolled weeks, plus the administrative expenses of managing the internal exchange program. It's a separate fee because it only pertains to points, not to any specific resort.

Other fees

  • Special assessment: A fee charged to all owners of a specific resort when a major unforeseen event occurs — hurricane damage to a coastal resort not fully covered by insurance, or a full roof replacement due to unexpected code enforcement, as examples. These are rare, but possible.
  • Exchange fees: The fee to transact an exchange within Interval. This is covered by Club Dues for points and enrolled inventory, if you're paying them.
  • Lock-off fee: If your week has the option to lock off and you select that option during booking, you'll be charged a lock-off fee.
