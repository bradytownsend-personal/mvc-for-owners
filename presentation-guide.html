Sales Presentations

What to know heading into the pitch

Whether you're an owner or a prospective one, here's what's worth knowing before you sit down for a presentation.

First, know that you'll get the incentive if you stay for the full presentation. If you're adamant about not purchasing and make that obvious, repeatedly, you may be let out early — but don't count on it.

Walking in for the first time

Walking into a sales presentation for the first time, you may be nervous — not knowing what's coming, not knowing how much it really costs to become an owner. Know that the sales associate you speak with is probably going to follow a standard procedure. You'll first be lulled into relaxation by sitting in a living-room-style area to get comfortable, and for them to pull out information they can use to later influence you in the sales pitch. The questions are calculated, used to get an idea of how much money you might be able to spend and how you might use MVC. Later, you'll be brought back to the sales office, where the real numbers start to come out. The information you provided earlier will be used to suggest you need MVC to fulfill your dreams. Remember that when you answer questions.

The points pitch

Since MVC transitioned to a points system, they'll most likely offer you a points purchase up front. Expect the number of points to start high so they can move down. Buying points directly comes with a significant purchase price — with current "incentives," expect the price per point to run around $17. That's $17k for every 1,000 points. Points must be purchased in increments of 250, with a minimum purchase of 1,500 (with some exceptions).

Review the Owner Benefit Levels to understand what your capabilities are based on how much you own. As an example, 1,500 points doesn't go too far, especially during peak season. Plus, at that owner level, your booking window is later than everyone else's. Shoulder and low seasons may work better, if that's what you're after.

If you say no to points, they may offer you a hybrid purchase — a week they have in inventory at a specific resort, plus a small number of points. The week would also be enrolled in Abound points. This option usually makes the overall price per point significantly lower than points alone.

The real yearly cost: maintenance fees

The yearly expense of ownership isn't something MVC really wants to dive into, so make sure you ask about it. Maintenance fees (MF) are how all owners pay for the annual upkeep of the resort, plus administrative overhead. Using points as the example, the current fee runs around $0.81 per point, or $810 per 1,000 points owned. The fee generally rises each year, 1–3% — plan that out over 10, 15, 20+ years. On top of the points fee, there's a "Club Dues" fee of a few hundred dollars per year. Also note that if you go for a hybrid purchase, you'll now pay the week's MF, the points' MF, and Club Dues.

Watch for exaggerations

Throughout this process, keep one thing in mind: they will say almost anything to get you to buy. We hear common lies or exaggerations of the truth from sales presentations. Will MVC buy back your ownership at a great price if you decide to get out later? No — for the most part, you pay them a fee to take it back. Will you be able to take that long-desired full family vacation to Hawaii with your 2,000-point purchase for a week? Maybe for a couple of days.

I have a friend who was given a 5-year plan of ownership when they purchased a hybrid deal that got them to 2,500 points, plus an Aruba Ocean Club 1-BDRM week. When they came home, we sat down and went through each year. Only one of the five years was even possible at their ownership level.

My recommendation

If you have any concerns about the offer and how you'd actually use MVC, I suggest walking away and doing more research. The offer will still be there after you've done that research and planned out what you truly should own.

Unless you've already done your research before heading into the presentation and know what you think you should own, just say no. Go back and do your homework first:

  1. Are the resorts in locations you actually want to travel to?
  2. Do you care about villa-style accommodations, or would a different style suit you better?
  3. Have you considered the resale market instead of buying direct?

On that last point: for points, the average resale price runs around $3.50 per point (instead of $17), plus MVC charges a $3-per-point transfer fee (minimum $3,000 transfer fee). So for roughly $6.50 or so per point, you can save a lot with no meaningful difference in what you actually own. Weeks can be very inexpensive on resale too, depending on the resort, size, view, and season — use rofr.net to get some sample data. The trade-off with resale weeks is that MVC won't allow you to enroll in points without a significant additional points purchase.

Let's break this down into a few different sections, based on your current ownership and how the presentation might go.

Currently a points owner

The sales associate will know exactly what you own and how you purchased it. If you purchased direct, they'll push that again, to get you more yearly points — a pretty simple discussion, since you already know the system. Maybe you do wish you owned more, to take more or different vacations you can't quite manage now. Make the right decision for you and your family; don't be pressured into purchasing more than you actually need. You should explore resale first to save money.

If you purchased resale already, you may be told you have a "weaker" ownership than if you'd purchased direct, and that buying direct will improve your capabilities. That's not true.

We've also heard a lot of offers to consolidate all your points into a single contract. There's no real benefit to you in doing that, and it may actually make things harder in the future if you decide to sell. Points are based on Beneficial Interests (BIs, 250 points each), but you're still selling a contract — and it's harder to sell a contract with 12 BIs (3,000 points) than one with 4 BIs (1,000 points).

Currently a week owner

First, the sales associate will check your recent reservation activity to see how you've actually been using your week. There are a couple of common sales tactics aimed at week owners.

The first is suggesting you deed back your week and convert everything to points, while also purchasing more points on top of it. You may even be told you're one of the last few owners who hasn't switched to points. But if you go back to your home resort for the week you own — and plan to keep doing that — converting it to points isn't a good idea. That removes your guaranteed week at that resort every year and puts you in the same position as every other points owner trying to book in. If you own a peak season, it'll be even harder to land that reservation through points alone. If you want the flexibility without giving up the guarantee, simply enroll your week into Abound instead, if you haven't already.

The second tactic is being told your fees are about to skyrocket as a week owner, and that you can save money by deeding the week back to MVC and buying points instead. This is aiming for essentially the same outcome as the first scenario — just a different story to get there.

Currently a hybrid owner (own week and points)

In this scenario, they could use any of the tactics already mentioned in either the week or points sections above. Keep in mind that you should only make a transaction you truly believe will benefit you and your family — if it doesn't sound right, it may not be right.

Many hybrid owners are already at a high ownership level, so the push will likely be to get you to the next tier up. If you don't need or want the additional points or week in your portfolio, don't do it. You already know the system — use that knowledge to make a smart decision, not a pressured one.
