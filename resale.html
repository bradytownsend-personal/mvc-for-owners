Exiting ownership

Resale & rental

Ready to sell, or just looking for another way to get value out of a week you're not using? Here's what the process actually involves.

One thing worth accepting early: resale value is almost never what you paid. Developer pricing covers a lot more than the real estate — marketing, sales staff, the presentation you sat through, ongoing program benefits. Resale value reflects mostly the underlying week or points themselves: season, size, resort demand, and current maintenance fees. That gap is normal, not a sign you got a bad deal originally.

When you're ready for a change

Your options

Selling isn't the only way out — and it isn't always the fastest.

Sell resale

List it and find a buyer

The traditional route — through a licensed timeshare resale broker or a resale marketplace. Expect it to take longer, and price for less, than developer sales did. Week prices vary significantly resort to resort and season to season. Points prices are normally about the same.

How resale works →

Deed-back / exit program

Hand it back

If you just want out and don't want to spend the time trying to sell to recover some of your money, the exit program may be an option. MVC may offer a formal deed-back or exit program for your ownership. Availability changes over time — worth asking directly rather than assuming it exists. Expect to pay around $400 per deed/contract to get out.

Rent it instead

Use it without selling

If you're not ready to sell, renting your week out for a season can offset maintenance fees while you decide — a lower-commitment option than a permanent sale. This option can also give you some time to figure out your best plan for next year.

What actually happens

The resale process

  1. List your week or points — with a broker, a resale marketplace, or an owner-to-owner listing, being upfront about maintenance fees and any current-year usage.
  2. Expect the resort or HOA to hold a right of first refusal in many cases — they may have a window to match an accepted offer before it can go to an outside buyer.
  3. Once a buyer's confirmed, the transfer typically goes through a licensed closing or title company, not a handshake — this protects both sides and creates a clean paper trail.
  4. Budget for transfer and closing costs, which are usually split or negotiated between buyer and seller.
  5. After closing, the new owner needs to be updated with the resort, the HOA, and the exchange company — don't assume this happens automatically.

Not ready to sell?

Renting your week as a middle ground

If you're on the fence, renting out a week you're not using is worth considering before you commit to a sale. It won't recover what you paid, but it can meaningfully offset that year's maintenance fee while you figure out whether to keep, sell, or exchange. PLEASE inform your renter that they are responsible for keeping the villa and resort clean, and they should follow posted rules while using your ownership.

Thinking about buying instead?

The same questions about fees, enrollment, and resale value are worth asking before you buy, not just before you sell.
