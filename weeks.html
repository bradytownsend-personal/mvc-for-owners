Ownership types

Week-based ownership

A deeded week ties your ownership to a specific resort, season, villa size, and view. Here's what that actually means when it's time to book.

A deeded week is a real estate interest — you own (or hold a right to use, depending on the resort's structure) a specific slice of a specific resort, year after year. With a week, the resort, the season, the villa size, and often the view are locked in at the time of purchase.

That specificity is the whole trade-off. If you love returning to the same resort around the same time every year, a deeded week gives you a predictable, known quantity — no chart to calculate. If your travel plans shift year to year, that same specificity can feel limiting.

Booking windows are very important, especially if you want the same dates everyone else wants. Know your options, 13-month or 12-month booking. Know your earliest reservation date by using the 'When can I reserve?' link under USE WEEK(S) on the owners page.

Owning a week gives you the right to book a week during your season, but it doesn't provide a guarantee. We see stories all the time where an owner really wanted specific dates, but didn't get them. Then they wait to figure it out later, but by the time they do, all the remaining inventory is already booked, and part of the season has already passed. MVC Owner Services can't create inventory that doesn't exist, so you're in a pickle. MVC usually tries to help by providing you with a Interval week, but it is totally up to them and what the circumstances are. Bottom line, book what you can, when you can.

Hot Topic

Should I enroll my week into points (Abound)?

Enrolling an eligible week into Abound is free, and it doesn't take anything away from you. Your week stays exactly as it is — same resort, same season, same right to book it every year, for as long as you own it. Enrollment doesn't convert anything and doesn't lock you into anything. All it does is add an option: the ability to use that week as Abound points instead, in any year you choose.

The one real cost is a new annual fee called Club Dues, on top of your existing maintenance fee — currently $255 and up, depending on where you fall in the Owner Benefit Levels. That's the actual trade-off. Nothing else about your ownership changes.

So the real question isn't "should I give up my week for points" — you're not giving up anything. It's simpler than that: is the flexibility worth an extra fee every year? If you go back to the same resort, the same time, year after year, and you're genuinely happy with that, enrollment probably isn't for you — you'd be paying for an option you'll likely never use. If you'd like the ability to try somewhere new, adjust your unit size, or mix points and week years as your plans change, that flexibility may well be worth it.

Either way, don't think of enrolling as giving up your week. You still own it. Points is just an option sitting alongside it — available only in the years you decide to use it.

Before deciding, it's worth reviewing the Owner Benefit Levels to see how your specific tier affects booking windows — that detail often matters more to the decision than the fee itself.

Once you own it

Six ways to use your week

Booking it yourself is only one option. Here's the full menu of what you can actually do with a week, year to year.

Use It

Book it, stay in it

The default path — reserve your week at your home resort and use it yourself. No coordinating with a third party, no exchange rates to think about, just your annual maintenance fee. Book when you can to secure the week and check-in day you want. If your plans change late, you're working with whatever's still open.

Exchange It

Trade it for somewhere else

Deposit your week with Interval International to book a different resort entirely. What you get back depends on your week's trading power — season, size, and how early you deposit. A high-demand week deposited early trades up nicely; a lower-demand week deposited late has slim options.

Split It

Two short stays for one

At resorts that offer it, you can split your week into a 3-night and a 4-night stay instead of one continuous 7 nights — same ownership, two trips. Not every resort or unit type supports it. If it's available to you, it'll show up when you book. Some Lockoff units allow you to split the unit and stay 2 weeks.

Rent It

Offset the fee without losing the year

Not going to make it this year? Renting your reservation to someone else can recoup some or all of that year's maintenance fee instead of the week going unused. Rentals typically move through resale/rental websites or owner Facebook groups — and renting comes with real scam risk, so vet whoever you're dealing with.

Elect Points (Abound)

Use this year's week as points instead

If your week is enrolled in Abound, you can elect — each year — to convert that year's week into Abound points instead of booking it as a week. That opens up the flexibility of the points system (different resorts, dates, unit sizes) for this year's allocation. It's a yearly choice, not a permanent switch.

Elect Bonvoy

Trade it for hotel-side flexibility

You can also convert your week's value into Marriott Bonvoy points instead — useful if you'd rather stay at a traditional Marriott hotel or use Bonvoy for flights and experiences. Exchange rates on this route tend to run worse than staying inside the MVC/Abound system, so it's worth comparing before making it your default.

Wondering how this compares to points?

Points ownership trades that fixed specificity for flexibility — different resort, different dates, different size, every year if you want.
